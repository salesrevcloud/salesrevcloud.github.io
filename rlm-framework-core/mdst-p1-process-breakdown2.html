Context: Where the Process Breakdown Fits

"Reconnaissance" or "scouting" is the absolute best analogy for the Pre-Discovery phase. If you just wander into the process forest without scouting it first, the business stakeholders will drag you deep into the weeds, and you will spend three weeks mapping how they order office supplies instead of fixing their quoting engine.

You Are Here (Process Breakdown)

Phase 1: The Aerial Recce

(Drawing the Map)

Before you interview a single sales rep, you sit with leadership to map out the high-level business capabilities. You are flying over the forest in a helicopter, identifying the boundaries of the forest, the major rivers, and the mountains. You are not mapping how they do it; you are just listing what they do to define the project perimeter.

Up Next

Phase 2: Boots on the Ground

(Walking the Trails)

Now that you have the map, you land the helicopter and walk the specific trails you outlined during the recce. This is where you document the "As-Is" pain points by mapping the literal steps and actors for each specific process.

Future State

Phase 3: Engineering the Highway

(The "To-Be")

You take the broken As-Is processes and redraw them to leverage the new technology (like Salesforce Revenue Cloud), ensuring the system handles the heavy lifting.

The Architect's Shield: The most powerful thing you can do as a consultant is control the scope. When a sales rep brings up a totally unrelated complaint during Discovery (e.g., "Our lead routing is broken!"), you pull out your Phase 1 map and say: "I hear you, but Lead Routing is outside our perimeter for this phase. Let's put it in the backlog."

The Core: RLM Process Breakdown (Levels 1 - 3)

We break down the business operations into three clear levels. Unlike traditional mapping that starts with a massive, generic "Quote-to-Cash" umbrella, we start directly with the strategic priorities finalized during the Executive Vision phase. This ensures every operational process we map is tethered to a top-level business goal.

Hierarchy What It Is Example (From Vision Rubric)
Level 1 (L1) Value Stream (The Priority): An executive priority translated into a measurable flow of business value. This comes directly from the finalized Vision Rubric. Standardize Recurring Revenue Handoffs
Level 2 (L2) Process Area: The major functional buckets required to deliver that L1 Value Stream. At this 10,000-foot view, the platform doesn't matter. Order Management, Billing & Invoicing
Level 3 (L3) Specific Process: The actual tasks to be accomplished. Knowing you are moving to Revenue Cloud changes your scouting here, as you target processes suited for native platform capabilities. Subscription Proration, Asset Generation

Action: Day 0 Alignment Workshop

Human psychology is funny: if you ask a room full of department heads to draw their business processes on a whiteboard, you get blank faces and awkward silence. But if you put a slightly incorrect process map on the screen, they will immediately jump out of their chairs to tell you exactly why you are wrong and how they actually do it. This is the "Straw Man" approach.

  • Duration: 2 Hours
  • Audience: Department Heads, Business Leads, and Process Owners (e.g., VP of Sales Ops, Deal Desk Director, Billing Manager).
  • Goal: Lock the L2-L3 Process boundaries beneath the executive L1 priorities, establishing the strict perimeter for the deep-dive Discovery phase.

Part 1: Anchoring to the Vision (30 Mins)

You do not start with technology. You start by playing back the L1 priorities their executives mandated in the Vision Phase.

  • The Mandate Lock: "Your leadership team identified 'Protecting Commercial Margins' as a top L1 priority. Our goal today is to map exactly which Deal Desk and Pricing processes fall under that umbrella so we can fix them."

Part 2: Tearing Down the Straw Man (60 Mins)

Project your pre-populated L2/L3 Process Breakdown on the screen and force binary decisions.

  • You: "Process 4.3 is 'Subscription Co-Terming.' Are we fixing that as part of this priority, or pushing it to Phase 2?"
  • Sales Ops Lead: "Push it. We don't have the data ready for that yet." (You mark it Out of Scope live on the screen).

Part 3: Assigning the "Boots on the Ground" (30 Mins)

For every In-Scope L3 process, demand a name for your upcoming detailed interviews.

  • You: "For 'Margin Floor Calculations' (Process 3.3), who is the absolute best person on your team who understands the current pain points?"

The Outcome: You walk out of this meeting with a locked scope, a clear Discovery Schedule, and business leads who feel confident because they simply had to edit your draft rather than stare at a blank page.

Optional Preparation: Document Harvesting & Comms

If you want to pre-fill your Process Breakdown before the Day 0 Workshop, you can attempt to harvest existing documentation. The golden rule is to never start with a blank page.

  • Industry Reference Models: Download standard maps from APQC or TM Forum to use as a baseline.
  • Artifact Harvesting: Ask the client's IT or PMO for the SOW, RFP, Org Charts, and Training Manuals. Mine these strictly for nouns and verbs.
  • System Snooping: Look at their current Salesforce schema (Custom Objects, Active Workflows) to get a literal list of automated processes.

Collaborative Email Request

If you choose to ask the client for documents beforehand, use this template. It positions you as a partner trying to save them time, rather than a consultant assigning homework.

Subject: Pre-Discovery Artifact Request: [Client Name] Revenue Cloud Transformation

Hi [Sponsor Name],

I am really looking forward to our upcoming official kickoff for the Revenue Cloud implementation.

To ensure we make the absolute best use of your team's time during the upcoming workshops, my team is currently putting together a baseline "Quote-to-Cash" process list. Rather than starting from scratch and taking up valuable meeting time with basic fact-finding, we want to map out as much of your current landscape as possible beforehand.

Could you or your PMO team please share any existing documentation you might have in the following areas by [Date]?

1. Strategic & Scope Alignment: The Business Case, KPIs, or high-level architecture diagrams.
2. Organizational Structure: Org charts for Sales, Deal Desk, and Finance, plus a list of key subject matter experts.
3. Process Artifacts: Any SOPs, Visio maps, or even a copy of the manual Excel spreadsheets your team currently uses for quoting/pricing.
4. System Context: A data dictionary or schema export of your current CRM/ERP setup.

Please do not worry if any of these documents are outdated or messy! We are simply looking for the broad strokes of how your business operates today to build our initial map. You can drop everything directly into this secure folder: [Link].

Let me know if you have any questions at all.
