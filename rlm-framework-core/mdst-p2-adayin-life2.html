A Day in the Life: Real-World Discovery

Managing the Chaos: Bridging SME Workshops into Agile Execution

The idea that Discovery is a neat, chronological march from "As-Is" to "To-Be" to "Gap Analysis" is a theoretical fallacy. In the real world, SMEs may not separate their current pain from their future desires.

In-the-Workshop "Micro-Cycle"

When you ask SMEs about how they handle a midterm amendment today (the As-Is), they will immediately complain about manual credit memos and jump straight into how they want it automated tomorrow (the To-Be)[cite: 5]. As an architect, when they ask, "Well, how does the system handle this?", they are forcing a real-time Gap Analysis—the "pre-gap".

Instead of fighting this, organize your sessions by "Commercial Thread," not by phase. Guide the SMEs through a continuous loop for every specific capability in a 10-minute window:

  • The Pain (As-Is): "We currently use three spreadsheets to calculate proration".
  • The Intent (To-Be): "We need the system to just do the math automatically when a rep changes the quantity".
  • The Pre-Gap (Platform Steering): "Revenue Cloud handles this out-of-the-box using the standard Pricing Procedure and Assetization rules, provided we standardize your service periods. Are you open to adopting the standard platform calculation?"

Platform-Guided Business Design

When business leaders ask for options, they are inviting you to shape their To-Be state to fit standard Salesforce architecture. If their To-Be aligns with standard functionality, you confirm it immediately. If it requires heavy customization, you use the pre-gap to challenge them: "We can build that custom logic, but if we adjust your process slightly to fit the standard Product Selling Model, you save 200 hours of development...".

The Unified Discovery Matrix

Because the conversation happens all at once, your documentation must capture it all at once. Ditch separate documents and use a unified matrix that captures the past, present, and the platform-specific future in a single row.

Commercial Process Current State (The Pain) Business Intent (To-Be Goal) RLM Platform Recommendation (Pre-Gap Steering) Architectural Gap / Risk
Mid-Term Add-On (Amendments) Sales reps manually calculate prorated amounts for new seats on a spreadsheet and submit a separate $0 order for tracking. System should automatically calculate the exact prorated cost for new seats based on the days remaining in the existing contract. Adopt the standard ProductSellingModel (Termed) and flag the product as "Asset-trackable". RLM's Asset Lifecycle engine will handle the co-termination and generate prorated Quote Lines. Low Gap, High Data Risk: RLM handles this natively, but we must ensure legacy contract data is migrated accurately into the new AssetStatePeriod object to serve as the baseline.
Renewal Uplift Pricing Finance applies a flat 5% uplift at renewal, but reps often override this manually or forget to apply it entirely. Automate a standard 5% price increase on all recurring software renewals, with manager approval required for overrides. Configure the PricingProcedure to look up a Renewal Uplift attribute. The standard Price Waterfall will calculate the Net Price automatically during the system-generated Renewal Quote. Process Gap: Business must agree to standardize the 5% rule across the catalog. Custom override logic will require a new step in the Pricing Procedure.
Hardware + SaaS Bundling Hardware is quoted in an ERP, while SaaS is quoted in CRM. Invoices are dispatched separately, causing customer confusion. Reps need to sell the physical sensor and the 12-month software subscription on the same quote and dispatch to billing together. Utilize Product Discovery to configure a bundle containing a One-Time PSM (Hardware) and a Termed PSM (SaaS). Both will flow through the same Quote-to-Order "Steel Thread" into a unified SalesOrder. Medium Gap: RLM easily handles mixed PSMs, but the external Billing Schedule integration (ERP) must be prepared to split the revenue recognition based on the different PSMs[cite: 5].

Why this format wins: It stops the "Customization Default" by anchoring the conversation in standard platform engines immediately. It exposes the real data/process risks rather than just asking "can the platform do it?". Most importantly, every row feeds directly into your Agile backlog as an End-to-End Revenue Thread Epic.
